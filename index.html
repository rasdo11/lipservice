The Injection Report
MRI Study Catches Filler Migration in the Act

What's going on: A study published this week in *Aesthetic Surgery Journal* used serial MRI imaging to track hyaluronic acid filler placement over 12 months in 30 patients who received midface injections. Researchers found measurable migration — an average of 4.2mm from the original injection site — in 73% of subjects, with infraorbital hollows being the most common destination for product originally placed in the medial cheek.

What it means: This isn't filler "spreading" from overfilling. It's gravity, facial movement, and tissue plane dynamics doing what physics does. The study suggests that deep bolus technique in the medial cheek may be more migration-prone than supraperiosteal microdroplet approaches, which stayed closer to placement.

What's next: Ask your injector whether they use bolus or microdroplet technique in your midface, and whether they've adjusted based on emerging migration data. This is the kind of study that changes protocols — slowly, but meaningfully.

The question to ask at your next appointment: "What technique do you use in the medial cheek, and have you seen the ASJ migration imaging study?" If they haven't, that's information too.

Put It In Your Mouth
High-Dose Cocoa Flavanols Just Got a Real Trial

What's going on: Results from the COSMOS ancillary study dropped this week in *The American Journal of Clinical Nutrition*, showing that 500mg daily cocoa flavanol supplementation over three years was associated with a 27% reduction in cardiovascular mortality among adults over 60 — and a secondary finding of improved skin elasticity scores in a subset of participants.

What that means: This isn't "dark chocolate is good for you" handwaving. It's a specific dose (500mg flavanols, which is roughly 10x what's in a square of 70% dark chocolate) showing vascular and dermal benefit in a large, placebo-controlled trial. You'd need a supplement to hit that number, not a candy bar.

This week's cheat meal: The brownie from Levain Bakery — the dark chocolate one specifically, because if you're going to eat your feelings about filler migration, at least make the cocoa count. Warm. No fork.

Lip Lab
Glossier Quietly Changed the Gloss Formula and We Noticed

What's going on: Glossier updated the formula of its original Lip Gloss this month with no press release — just a quiet ingredient list swap spotted by cosmetics chemists on Reddit. Out: ethylhexyl palmitate (the slightly sticky plasticizer). In: polybutene and a meadowfoam seed oil blend.

Why it matters: The old formula was fine but polarizing — too sticky for anyone who'd graduated to Dior or Fenty. The reformulation is clearly chasing a cushion-gel texture that's closer to the K-beauty gloss trend without abandoning Glossier's wet-look DNA.

The verdict: Smoother, less tacky, slightly less glossy. If you left Glossier Gloss for being clingy, this is your reentry point. If you loved the original's mirror finish, you'll notice the difference.

The new formula is already shipping — no old-stock distinction on the site. Check your ingredient list: if polybutene is listed, you've got the update.
