The Injection Report
The Post-GLP-1 Filler Recalculation Is Officially Underway

What's going on: A new survey published this week in *Aesthetic Surgery Journal* found that 67% of facial plastic surgeons have modified their filler protocols for patients on GLP-1 receptor agonists, with most reducing total volume by 20–40% compared to pre-weight-loss plans. The issue: rapid fat pad loss in the buccal and malar regions is changing where filler sits, how it migrates, and how long it lasts — and the corrections patients got 18 months ago are now sitting wrong on a thinner face.

What it means: When you lose significant facial volume quickly, existing HA filler doesn't just stay put and look proportional. It can appear more prominent, shift position as surrounding tissue deflates, or create visible asymmetry that wasn't there at a higher weight. Some injectors are recommending partial dissolution with hyaluronidase before re-injecting at adjusted volumes and new vectors — essentially a reset.

What's next: If you're on a GLP-1 and have filler from before your weight loss, book a reassessment — not a touch-up. Ask specifically about filler mapping relative to your current fat pad volume, not your old one.

Ask your injector: 'Are you planning this for my face now or the face I had when you first injected me?' If they can't answer that clearly, find someone who can.

Put It In Your Mouth
High-Dose Olive Oil and the Skin Barrier Study That Has Everyone Pouring

What's going on: A randomized controlled trial out of the University of Barcelona, published this week in *Nutrients*, gave one group 50 mL of extra-virgin olive oil daily (about 3.5 tablespoons) for 12 weeks and measured transepidermal water loss, skin elasticity, and hydration. The EVOO group showed a 14% improvement in skin hydration and statistically significant improvement in elasticity versus the control group, who got refined sunflower oil.

What that means: The oleocanthal and hydroxytyrosol in high-quality EVOO are potent anti-inflammatories that appear to strengthen skin barrier function from the inside. This isn't news to anyone who's been cooking Mediterranean, but the dose and the controlled design make this one harder to dismiss.

This week's cheat meal: A thick slice of sourdough absolutely drowned in high-polyphenol EVOO with flaky salt and a side of burrata. Call it a skin treatment.

What to cut: The refined seed oil you're using for salad dressing — it was the control group for a reason.

Lip Lab
Pat McGrath Quietly Reformulated MatteTrance and We Need to Talk

What's going on: Pat McGrath Labs rolled out updated MatteTrance lipstick packaging this month, and buried in the rebrand is a reformulation — new batch codes confirm an adjusted ingredient list that swaps synthetic beeswax for a plant-derived alternative and adds squalane. The texture has shifted.

Why it matters: The original MatteTrance was a true matte that set and didn't move, which is exactly why people loved or hated it. The squalane addition makes it more comfortable but slightly less transfer-proof. If you were a Flesh 3 loyalist who liked that your lipstick survived a nap, the new version is a different product wearing the same name.

The verdict: More wearable, less iconic. The new formula is genuinely good — creamy application, real pigment density, no detectable feathering after four hours. But if you wanted the original's brutal staying power, stock up on old-batch inventory while it exists.

Check the batch code on the bottom of the tube. If it starts with '24' or earlier, it's the old formula. '25' batches are the reformulation.
