The Injection Report
Letybo's Phase III Data Just Made Every Other Toxin Nervous

What's going on: Hugel's letibotulinumtoxinA (Letybo) posted topline Phase III results this week showing non-inferiority to onabotulinumtoxinA for glabellar lines — with a median duration hitting 24 weeks in a significant subset of patients. The Korean-made toxin is already approved in 73 countries and is now on track for U.S. FDA submission by Q4 2025.

What it means: Twenty-four weeks is not the new baseline — it's the high end of their responder curve — but it's enough to reframe the conversation about treatment intervals. The formulation uses a 900kDa complex similar to Botox, so the switch wouldn't feel dramatic in-chair, but fewer visits per year changes the math on annual spend.

What's next: Ask your injector if they're tracking Letybo. If they haven't heard of it, that tells you something about how current their continuing education is.

If Letybo gets FDA clearance at its expected price point, annual neurotoxin costs could drop 15–20% for maintenance patients. Start the conversation now.

Put It In Your Mouth
High-Dose Cocoa Flavanols Are Doing Skin Things Again

What's going on: A randomized controlled trial published this week in *The Journal of Nutrition* found that 500mg of cocoa flavanols daily for 12 weeks improved skin elasticity by 9% and reduced transepidermal water loss compared to placebo. The cohort was women 40–60, which is rare and useful — most flavanol studies use 20-somethings whose skin forgives everything.

What that means: You cannot eat your way to a facelift, but 500mg of cocoa flavanols is a meaningful dose with consistent data behind it now. That's roughly 30g of high-flavanol dark chocolate or a targeted supplement like CocoaVia.

This week's cheat meal: A double-thick slice of flourless chocolate cake from that bakery you've been pretending you don't know the hours of. Call it a clinical intervention.

Lip Lab
Dior Lip Oil Got a Quiet Reformulation and We Have Questions

What's going on: Eagle-eyed Reddit users flagged this week that Dior Addict Lip Glow Oil's ingredient list has changed — cherry seed oil moved down the INCI order, and hydrogenated polyisobutene moved up, which functionally makes this more of a gloss base with oil marketing.

Why it matters: The original formula's appeal was actual oil-phase hydration sitting on the lip. Moving to a polyisobutene-forward base gives you shine and slip but less of the emollient benefit. It's not worse — it's different, and they didn't tell you.

The verdict: If you loved the original for moisture, you'll notice. If you loved it for the glossy wash of color, you probably won't. Either way, a $40 product should announce its reformulations.

Check the batch code on your current tube at checkcosmetic.net — if it was manufactured before March 2025, you have the old formula. Stock accordingly.
