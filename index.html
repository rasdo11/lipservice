The Injection Report
The MRI Receipts on Filler Migration Are In

What's going on: A study published this week in *Aesthetic Surgery Journal* used MRI imaging on 67 patients who'd had hyaluronic acid filler placed in the midface 12–36 months prior. In over 70% of cases, filler had migrated beyond its original injection plane — most commonly inferiorly toward the nasolabial fold and anteriorly toward the infraorbital area. This isn't a scare piece; it's the first large-cohort MRI confirmation of what injectors have suspected clinically.

What it means: Migration isn't the same as a bad result. Some displacement is gravitational, some is mechanical (every time you chew, you're nudging product). The study found that higher volumes per session and bolus technique — depositing one large depot rather than microdroplets — correlated with more displacement. Cannula-placed, layered microdroplet filler stayed put more reliably.

What's next: If you're due for a midface touch-up, ask your injector whether they use bolus or serial puncture technique and what volume they're placing per session. Smaller sessions, more frequently, appears to win.

Ask your injector: 'What's your per-session volume cap for the midface, and are you placing bolus or microdroplets?' If they can't answer specifically, that's your answer.

Put It In Your Mouth
High-Dose Omega-3s and the Lip Barrier You Didn't Know You Had

What's going on: A randomized controlled trial out of Seoul National University, published Monday in the *British Journal of Dermatology*, found that 12 weeks of 2g/day EPA-dominant omega-3 supplementation significantly improved transepidermal water loss (TEWL) on the vermilion border — the lip line — by 22% compared to placebo. It's the first study to measure omega-3's effect on lip-specific barrier function rather than general facial skin.

What that means: Your lip skin has no sebaceous glands, so it's entirely dependent on systemic lipid delivery for barrier repair. The study suggests EPA specifically — not DHA — drives the ceramide synthesis that keeps lips from becoming a moisture dead zone.

This week's cheat meal: A tin of high-quality sardines on sourdough with pickled shallots and Maldon salt. You're getting 1.8g of EPA per tin and you'll feel like you're in Lisbon. No apologies.

What to cut: If your omega-3 supplement is DHA-dominant (flip the bottle — most are), it may not be doing the same work for your skin barrier. Look for EPA-first formulations.

Lip Lab
Patrick Ta Reformulated the Major Headlines Lip Crème and We Have Feelings

What's going on: Patrick Ta quietly updated the Major Headlines Double-Take Crème & Powder Blush Lip Duo this week — new formula, same packaging, no announcement. Reddit caught it first. The crème side now contains polyglutamic acid instead of the original sodium hyaluronate base.

Why it matters: Polyglutamic acid holds roughly four times the moisture of hyaluronic acid by weight and forms a film rather than absorbing into skin. On lips, that translates to more surface-level plumping and longer wear — but it also means a tackier texture that plays differently under gloss.

The verdict: The new formula wears 30+ minutes longer and photographs better. But if you loved the original's slip, this is a different product. Best on bare lips or over pencil — skip the gloss layer.

If you're repurchasing Patrick Ta's lip crème, check the batch. New formula performs differently under gloss — wear it alone or over liner for best results.
