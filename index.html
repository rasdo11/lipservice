The Injection Report
New MRI Study Shows Filler Migrating Way Beyond the Injection Site

What's going on: A study published this week in *Aesthetic Surgery Journal* used high-resolution MRI to track hyaluronic acid filler in 47 patients who'd received midface injections 6–24 months prior. Researchers found filler deposits in the periorbital region, the nasolabial fold, and — in three cases — along vascular pathways leading toward the infraorbital foramen, areas nowhere near the original injection site.

What it means: Filler migration isn't new gossip, but imaging confirmation at this resolution is. The study suggests that even properly placed HA filler can shift along tissue planes over time, particularly in patients with higher facial mobility. The three vascular-adjacent cases didn't result in complications, but they raise real questions about long-term depot behavior that most consent forms don't address.

What's next: Ask your injector whether they've adjusted volume or placement based on emerging migration data. If they haven't read the study, that's information too.

The study found filler displacement in 72% of patients scanned at 12+ months. If you're overdue for a check-in with your injector, this is your reason to book one — not for more filler, for imaging.

Put It In Your Mouth
Watermelon Just Got a Collagen Upgrade (Sort Of)

What's going on: A study out of Chulalongkorn University published Monday in *Food Chemistry* found that citrulline — the amino acid watermelon is loaded with — significantly boosted type I collagen synthesis in human dermal fibroblasts at concentrations achievable through diet. Researchers noted a 23% increase in procollagen output compared to controls over 72 hours.

What that means: You're not going to eat your way to a facelift, but citrulline's role in arginine metabolism and nitric oxide production means it supports both collagen synthesis and blood flow to skin. Two cups of watermelon gives you roughly 500mg of citrulline — right in the study's effective range.

This week's cheat meal: A thick slice of high-summer watermelon with crumbled feta, mint, and a stupid amount of flaky salt. Eaten standing at the counter. No plate required.

This Week’s Cheat Meal

Citrulline from whole watermelon outperformed the isolated supplement form in the study — likely due to co-occurring lycopene and vitamin C. Eat the fruit, skip the capsule.
