The Injection Report
The Peptide That Wants to Replace Your Top-Up

What's going on: OneSkin this week published 12-week clinical trial results for its new topical OS-O2 peptide, showing a measurable delay in the return of dynamic lines post-neuromodulator treatment. The study — small, 48 participants, split-face — found subjects using the peptide serum retained smoother glabellar lines an average of 3.2 weeks longer than the control side. The brand is positioning it as a "Botox extender," which is a phrase that will make your injector either very interested or very annoyed.

What it means: Copper peptides have solid evidence for collagen stimulation and wound healing, but claiming to functionally extend neuromodulator results is a different beast. The mechanism here is about skin quality — firmer, thicker dermal tissue shows less wrinkling even as the toxin wears off. It's not replacing your units; it's making the surface more cooperative. Your injector will still see you on schedule.

What's next: If you're already on a peptide serum, this isn't a reason to switch — it's a reason to ask your provider whether your current routine is optimized for the weeks between appointments. The question to bring: "Should my topical protocol change based on where I am in my injection cycle?"

Ask your injector: "Am I using actives that complement my neuromodulator timeline, or am I just layering products randomly?" Most are — and most injectors have an opinion about it if you ask.

Put It In Your Mouth
The FDA Just Reminded You That Collagen Powder Isn't Regulated Like You Think

What's going on: The FDA published updated draft guidance this week clarifying that collagen and biotin supplements marketed with structure/function claims still don't require pre-market approval — but brands making skin-specific efficacy claims ("clinically proven to reduce wrinkles") without a disclaimer are now on notice. Two major supplement brands received warning letters the same day, though the FDA hasn't named them publicly yet.

What that means: Your collagen powder might be fine. It also might be sawdust with good branding. The issue isn't collagen peptides as a concept — hydrolyzed collagen has decent absorption data — it's that nobody is checking whether what's on the label matches what's in the tub. If your brand doesn't do third-party testing (NSF, USP, ConsumerLab), you're trusting vibes.

This week's cheat meal: The lobster roll at Luke's just came back in summer menu rotation and I'm not going to pretend I need a nutritional justification. Butter, split-top bun, a squeeze of lemon. Collagen is in the shell broth if you need to tell yourself something.

Lip Lab
Merit's Reformulated Lip Oil Dropped Without Fanfare and It's the One

What's going on: Merit quietly reformulated its Shade Slick Lip Oil this month, swapping out the old jojoba-heavy base for a blend of meadowfoam seed and squalane. The brand didn't do a big launch — it just showed up on shelves with updated INCI lists and the same packaging.

Why it matters: Meadowfoam seed oil has an unusually long-chain fatty acid profile, which means better occlusion without that thick, sticky-gloss feeling. The squalane keeps it from settling into lip lines. It's a smarter formula than the original and a direct shot at the Dior Lip Oil's $40 price point — Merit's is $26.

The verdict: Sheer wash of color, zero grit, doesn't migrate. The shade "Marrakech" is the one for summer — a warm terracotta that reads as "my lips if they had their life together."

If you bounced off Merit's lip oil the first time because it was too slippery, the reformulation fixed exactly that. Worth a second try, especially "Marrakech."
