The Injection Report
The Filler That Breaks Up With You First

What's going on: Juvéderm's parent company Allergan filed new clinical data this week for a modified HA filler with a built-in degradation timeline — engineered to lose volume predictably over 8 months rather than the usual unpredictable 9-to-18-month fade. The Phase III results, presented at the Aesthetic Meeting in Vancouver, showed 94% of patients returned to baseline by month 9 with no residual migration. What it means: This is the industry's answer to the filler accumulation panic. Stacking — where residual product builds up from repeated sessions — is real, and MRI studies have confirmed HA can persist far longer than package inserts claim. A filler with a hard expiration date would fundamentally change the risk calculus for maintenance patients. What's next: No FDA submission timeline yet, but Allergan indicated late 2025. Ask your injector if they're tracking this — it tells you whether they're reading the literature or just the sales reps' slides.

If your injector can't explain the difference between enzymatic degradation and mechanical breakdown of HA, find one who can. That distinction is about to matter a lot.

Put It In Your Mouth
Fish Oil Just Got Interesting Again

What's going on: A randomized trial published Monday in the Journal of the American Academy of Dermatology found that 2g/day of EPA-dominant omega-3 supplementation for 12 weeks significantly improved skin hydration and reduced transepidermal water loss in women 35-55 — the first well-powered RCT to show a measurable dermal effect at a specific dose. What that means: Previous fish oil-skin studies were small, used mixed EPA/DHA ratios, and measured vibes. This one used objective corneometry and had 240 participants. The key: EPA-dominant, not DHA-dominant. Most supplements on shelves are the opposite ratio. This week's cheat meal: The smoked salmon toast at Russ & Daughters — pillowy sable, cream cheese, and enough omega-3s to call it dermatological research.

Lip Lab
Saie Just Reformulated Its Bestseller and Nobody's Talking About It

What's going on: Saie quietly updated the formula for its Really Great Gloss this month — swapping synthetic colorants for mineral pigments and removing BHT, per updated ingredient lists spotted on Ulta this week. Why it matters: BHT is a preservative that's been under EU scrutiny for endocrine activity at high exposure levels. Removing it signals Saie is chasing EU compliance ahead of any US regulatory push — and the new formula uses iron oxides that shift the shade range slightly warmer across the board. The verdict: The reformulation is smoother, less sticky, and the vanilla-adjacent scent is finally gone. Shade 'Coy' now reads as a true rosy nude instead of the pink-leaning version from before. Worth a re-buy if you wrote it off.

If you loved the old shade 'Cheeky,' the new version runs about one shade warmer — swatch before committing.
