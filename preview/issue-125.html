The Injection Report
The Tyndall Effect Isn't a Vibe — It's a Complication

What's going on: A retrospective published this week in *Dermatologic Surgery* analyzed 312 cases of Tyndall effect — that bluish discoloration caused by superficially placed hyaluronic acid filler — and found that 68% of patients were told by their original injector that the tint was 'temporary' or 'normal settling.' It isn't. The study also flagged that certain HA fillers with larger particle sizes placed in the vermillion border are disproportionately responsible.

What it means: Tyndall effect means filler is too shallow, full stop. It doesn't resolve with time; it resolves with hyaluronidase or, in some cases, needle aspiration. The study's authors are pushing for Tyndall-specific documentation in complication reporting, which would give us actual prevalence data for the first time.

What's next: If you have any bluish tint at injection sites — lips, tear troughs, nasolabial folds — bring it up at your next appointment and specifically ask whether hyaluronidase correction is appropriate. Do not let anyone tell you to wait it out.

Ask your injector: 'At what depth are you placing this product, and what's your protocol if I develop Tyndall effect?' If they look confused, leave.

Put It In Your Mouth
The Watermelon-Lycopene Study Is Real (but Read the Fine Print)

What's going on: A University of São Paulo trial published Monday in *The Journal of Nutrition* found that participants who consumed 300g of fresh watermelon daily for six weeks showed a 17% increase in skin lycopene concentration and measurable improvement in UV-induced erythema recovery. Beauty Twitter ran with 'watermelon is sunscreen,' which — no.

What that means: Lycopene is a legitimate photoprotective antioxidant, but dietary intake supports your SPF, it doesn't replace it. The study's standout finding is actually the dose: you need about two cups daily, consistently, to see dermal benefit. Supplemental lycopene didn't perform as well as whole fruit.

This week's cheat meal: A slab of high-hydration focaccia from Bread Ahead's new NYC outpost, dragged through burrata and too much olive oil. No notes.

Lip Lab
The Lip Oil Correction Is Here

What's going on: Glossier quietly reformulated Lip Gloss this week — and the new version is functionally a lip oil, not a gloss. Meanwhile, both Merit and Ilia have pulled their lip oil SKUs from Sephora shelves for 'packaging updates,' which insiders say is code for formula rework after customer complaints about bleeding and feathering.

Why it matters: The lip oil boom created a category with zero standards — some were genuinely nourishing (meadowfoam, jojoba), others were mineral oil in a doe-foot applicator charging $28. The market is self-correcting.

The verdict: Glossier's new formula is excellent — lightweight, non-sticky, actual squalane and rosehip in the base. If you abandoned them after the original reformulation drama, this is your re-entry point.

If your lip oil bleeds into fine lines within an hour, the base is too thin and the emollients are wrong. Switch or layer a lip liner underneath.
