The Injection Report
The Largest Counterfeit Filler Seizure in FDA History

What's going on: The FDA announced this week that U.S. Customs seized over 30,000 units of counterfeit hyaluronic acid filler at ports in Los Angeles and New York — the agency's largest single interception of fraudulent injectables. The products were branded to mimic Juvéderm and Restylane packaging but originated from unlicensed facilities in China and Turkey, routed through Mexico and Canada. Some units from the same supply chain had already reached med spas in Texas and Florida before the seizure.

What it means: Counterfeit HA fillers aren't just diluted — they can contain industrial-grade silicone, non-sterile saline, or unknown polymers that cause granulomas, vascular occlusion, and tissue necrosis. Board-certified injectors source directly from Allergan or Galderma; the counterfeit pipeline feeds discount med spas and mobile injectors who buy off gray-market wholesalers. If you've ever been injected somewhere that couldn't show you the sealed box with a matching lot number, this is the risk you took.

What's next: Ask your injector to open the sealed product in front of you — every time, not just the first visit. If they hesitate or say it's "already drawn up," leave.

The FDA is urging patients to request lot numbers and verify them on the manufacturer's website. Allergan has a batch verification tool at AllerganAesthetics.com — bookmark it.

Put It In Your Mouth
The Collagen-Protein Connection Finally Has Numbers

What's going on: A study published this week in *Nutrients* tracked 120 women aged 35–55 over 12 weeks and found that those consuming 15g+ of dietary collagen peptides daily alongside at least 1.2g/kg body weight of total protein showed measurably improved skin elasticity and hydration versus the collagen-only and protein-only control groups. The combination effect was statistically significant — neither alone hit the same markers.

What that means: Your collagen supplement isn't useless, but it's underperforming if you're not hitting your protein floor. The mechanism is straightforward: collagen peptides signal fibroblast activity, but fibroblasts need amino acid availability to actually build. Undereating protein cancels the signal.

Lip Lab
Merit's Reformulated Lip Oil Is a Different Product Now

What's going on: Merit quietly reformulated its Shade Slick lip oil this month — new version started shipping last week with updated INCI lists. The brand confirmed the change to *Allure* but hasn't made a public announcement.

Why it matters: The old formula used a synthetic squalane base; the new one swaps in meadowfoam seed oil and adds a konjac-derived film-former that extends wear from roughly 90 minutes to closer to three hours in my testing. They also cut the fragrance, which was the number-one complaint in Sephora reviews.

The verdict: Better formula, same price ($24). The finish is slightly less glossy and more satin — if you liked the wet look, you'll notice. If you wanted something that survives a coffee, this is it.

Check the back of the tube: if the first ingredient is squalane, you have the old formula. Meadowfoam seed oil = the new one.
