The Injection Report
A Topical Peptide Is Posting Botox-Adjacent Numbers

What's going on: OneSkin published peer-reviewed clinical data this week showing its proprietary OS-01 peptide reduced crow's feet depth by 31% over 12 weeks in a 50-person RCT — numbers that creep into low-dose neurotoxin territory. The study, in the *Journal of Cosmetic Dermatology*, used 3D skin imaging and independent grading.

What it means: Nobody serious is calling this a Botox replacement. But 31% wrinkle depth reduction from a topical is unusually strong clinical data — most peptide serums post 10–15% in similar trials. The mechanism is senolytic (clearing damaged cells) rather than paralytic, so it's working a completely different pathway than neurotoxins. That's what makes it interesting, not competitive.

What's next: If you're already on a neurotoxin schedule, this isn't a reason to stop. If you're in the "not ready for needles" camp or spacing appointments further apart, ask your derm whether a senolytic peptide could extend your interval.

The 31% figure is for crow's feet specifically. Forehead and glabellar lines weren't measured — don't extrapolate this to your whole face.

Put It In Your Mouth
High-Dose Olive Oil Is a Collagen Story Now

What's going on: A meta-analysis published this week in *Nutrients* pooled data from nine trials and found that daily intake of 25–40mL of extra virgin olive oil (roughly 2–3 tablespoons) significantly improved skin elasticity and hydration markers over 8–12 weeks, with the effect driven primarily by hydroxytyrosol's action on MMP-1 inhibition — the enzyme that breaks down collagen.

What that means: You already knew olive oil was good. What's new is the dose-response data: below 20mL/day, skin outcomes were statistically insignificant. The threshold matters. Pour generously.

This week's cheat meal: The burrata at Via Carota, split down the middle with a pool of Castelvetrano oil you're now medically justified in drinking.

This Week’s Cheat Meal
The effective dose is 2–3 tablespoons daily of EVOO — not olive oil blend, not cooking-grade. Cold-pressed, high-polyphenol. The hydroxytyrosol content varies wildly by brand.

Lip Lab
Glossier Quietly Reformulated Ultralip and Hoped You Wouldn't Notice

What's going on: Glossier updated the Ultralip formula this month with no announcement — eagle-eyed Reddit users flagged a new ingredient deck listing hydrogenated polyisobutene replacing the original's meadowfoam seed oil base, plus the addition of squalane.

Why it matters: Hydrogenated polyisobutene gives a glossier, more slip-forward finish but doesn't have the occlusive staying power of meadowfoam. If your Ultralip suddenly felt thinner and more balm-like, you're not imagining it. The squalane addition suggests they're compensating for lost moisture lock.

The verdict: New formula is prettier on application, worse at 3 hours. If you were an Ultralip loyalist for the wear time, you have a legitimate grievance.

Compare your tube's batch code to the ingredient list on Glossier's current site — if they don't match, you've got old stock, which is arguably the better product right now.
