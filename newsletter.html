The Injection Report
New Data on Hyaluronidase Dosing Makes Filler Dissolving Less of a Guessing Game

What's going on: A study published this week in the *Journal of Cosmetic Dermatology* mapped optimal hyaluronidase concentrations against seven leading HA fillers, finding that dose-response curves vary dramatically by crosslinking density. Voluma required nearly 3x the units of Restylane Silk for equivalent dissolution. Until now, most injectors were titrating by feel.

What it means: If you've ever had filler dissolved and the result was uneven — too much gone here, a lump still there — this is why. The field has been working with rough estimates. This paper gives injectors a filler-specific dosing framework, which means fewer correction appointments and less collateral damage to your own native hyaluronic acid.

What's next: Ask your injector if they adjust hyaluronidase dosing by product. If they say "I use the same dose for everything," that's your cue to keep looking.

The study found Juvéderm Voluma needed 150+ units of hyaluronidase per 0.1 mL for full dissolution — nearly triple what softer fillers required. Product-specific dosing isn't optional anymore.

Put It In Your Mouth
Cardiology Just Gave Cocoa Flavanols a Standing Ovation

What's going on: The American College of Cardiology's annual meeting this week spotlighted follow-up data from the COSMOS trial confirming that 500 mg/day of cocoa flavanols reduced cardiovascular events by 27% in adults over 60 — with particular benefit for those with low baseline fruit and vegetable intake. The supplement, not the Hershey bar.

What that means: Flavanols improve endothelial function, which is a fancy way of saying they help your blood vessels stay flexible. Flexible vessels = better circulation = the thing that actually gives your skin that "glow" people spend $400 on LED masks chasing.

This week's cheat meal: A thick slice of flourless chocolate cake from wherever does it best near you. The flavanol content is negligible but the serotonin is not.

What to cut: Cocoa supplements with added cadmium — ConsumerLab flagged two more brands this week for heavy metal contamination above California Prop 65 thresholds.

This Week’s Cheat Meal
Flourless chocolate cake — dense, fudgy, unapologetic. Pair with espresso. Do not pair with guilt.
Look for cocoa flavanol supplements standardized to 500 mg flavanols per dose — and check ConsumerLab's updated list before you buy. Not all cocoa extracts are tested for heavy metals.

Lip Lab
Clinique Almost Lipstick Got a Quiet Reformulation and Nobody Told You

What's going on: Clinique's Almost Lipstick in Black Honey — the one that went viral in 2021 and again in 2023 — was quietly reformulated in the latest production batch. Updated INCI lists show the removal of carmine and the addition of synthetic iron oxides and Red 28 Lake.

Why it matters: Carmine is a known contact allergen for a small but real subset of people, and its removal makes this formula vegan for the first time. But Red 28 Lake is a fluorescein derivative that some dermatologists flag for photosensitivity on lips — relevant if you're a daily wearer.

The verdict: The finish is slightly more sheer and the color leans cooler. If you loved the original warmth, you'll notice. If you just want the "my lips but haunted" effect, you're fine.

If you're a daily Black Honey wearer and photosensitive, patch-test the new batch — Red 28 Lake can cause UV-reactive irritation on lip skin.
