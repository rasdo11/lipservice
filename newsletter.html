The Injection Report
Your Decade-Old Filler Didn't Dissolve — It Relocated

What's going on: A retrospective imaging study published this week in *Aesthetic Surgery Journal* analyzed MRI scans of 98 patients who'd received HA filler injections between 2 and 12 years prior. Researchers found persistent filler material in 87% of cases — and in 41%, filler had migrated beyond the original injection site, most commonly settling into the periosteal plane along the maxilla. Translation: your old lip and nasolabial filler may be sitting on your cheekbone now.

What it means: This isn't about bad injectors — even well-placed product moves over time as facial fat pads shift and gravity does its thing. The study reinforces what top injectors have been whispering: HA filler doesn't reliably "dissolve in 6–12 months" the way we've been told. It persists, it migrates, and it accumulates if you're layering new product over old.

What's next: If you've been getting filler for 5+ years without a baseline MRI, this is your nudge. Ask your injector about ultrasound-guided assessment before your next syringe — it's faster and cheaper than MRI and can show retained product in real time.

Before your next filler appointment, ask: "Can we do an ultrasound check for retained product first?" Any injector worth seeing will say yes.

Put It In Your Mouth
Lycopene Just Got a Glow-Up in the Literature

What's going on: A meta-analysis out of Seoul National University published Monday in *Nutrients* pooled data from 14 RCTs and found that supplemental lycopene at doses above 12mg/day significantly reduced UV-induced erythema and improved skin elasticity markers — with the strongest effects showing up at 8+ weeks of consistent intake. This isn't new-new, but the effect size at higher doses surprised even the authors.

What that means: Twelve milligrams is roughly what you'd get from two cups of cooked tomatoes or about 1.5 cups of watermelon. Supplementation works, but so does just eating red and pink produce consistently. The internal photoprotection angle is real — not sunscreen-replacement real, but measurably helpful.

This week's cheat meal: A proper caprese with peak-season heirloom tomatoes, burrata that costs too much, and enough olive oil to make your cardiologist proud. You're getting lycopene and you're not sorry about it.

Lip Lab
Glossier Reformulated Ultralip and We Have Feelings

What's going on: Glossier quietly updated the Ultralip formula this month — new packaging dropped Tuesday with a revised ingredient list that swaps meadowfoam seed oil for a blend of squalane and jojoba. The texture is noticeably thinner.

Why it matters: Meadowfoam was doing heavy lifting in the original — it's why Ultralip felt more like a balm-gloss hybrid than a sheer lipstick. The new version leans glossier (lowercase g), sheerer, and less emollient. If you loved the original's weight on your lips, stock up on old-formula tubes while they're still floating around retailers.

The verdict: The new formula is fine. It's pretty. It's also a different product wearing the same name. If you want the slip without the substance, you'll like it. If Ultralip was your "one lip product on a desert island" — you just lost your island pick.

Old-formula Ultralip tubes are still available at some Sephora locations and on resale. The packaging is matte; the new one is glossy. Check before you buy.
