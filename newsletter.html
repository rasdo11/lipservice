The Injection Report
HA Filler and the Immune Response Nobody Warned You About

What's going on: A paper published this week in the *Journal of Cosmetic Dermatology* used PET-CT imaging to show persistent low-grade inflammatory activity around hyaluronic acid filler deposits up to 3+ years post-injection. The inflammation was subclinical — patients had no visible swelling or pain — but the immune markers were unmistakably elevated. The study was small (n=31) but the imaging was unusually precise.

What it means: This isn't "filler is dangerous" — it's "filler is not biologically inert the way we've been told." HA has always been marketed as naturally occurring and fully resorbable, but this data suggests the cross-linking agents used to make filler last may be what the immune system keeps reacting to. Your body knows something foreign is there even when you've forgotten about it.

What's next: Ask your injector what cross-linking technology their preferred filler uses — BDDE concentration varies meaningfully across brands. If you're stacking filler over old filler annually, this is worth a real conversation about dissolving and resetting.

The filler itself may not be the problem — the cross-linker is. Ask your injector: "What's the BDDE concentration in what you're using, and does it matter for my history?"

Put It In Your Mouth
Watermelon Just Got a Collagen Origin Story

What's going on: A study out of Chulalongkorn University published this week in *Food Chemistry* found that citrulline — abundant in watermelon and its rind — significantly upregulated procollagen synthesis in human dermal fibroblasts at concentrations achievable through dietary intake. Not supplement-grade megadosing. Actual food.

What that means: Citrulline converts to arginine, which supports nitric oxide production and blood flow to the dermis. The collagen angle is newer and more direct than the "eat for circulation" story we've had before. This is one of the first papers to show procollagen stimulation from a food-derived amino acid at realistic doses.

This week's cheat meal: A thick slice of high-summer watermelon with Tajín, flaky salt, and a squeeze of lime over crushed ice. If you're not eating the white part near the rind, you're leaving the citrulline on the table.

What to cut: Those $60 "beauty collagen" powders with 200mg of vitamin C and pixie dust. You're better off eating actual food with actual amino acid precursors.

Lip Lab
Pat McGrath Reformulated Her Lip Fetish — and Changed the Wax System

What's going on: Pat McGrath Labs quietly updated the MatteTrance formula this month, and beauty chemists on Reddit clocked the change before the brand said a word. The new INCI list swaps synthetic beeswax for a plant-derived candelilla-rice bran hybrid and adds squalane where there used to be dimethicone.

Why it matters: This isn't just a clean-beauty rebrand. Candelilla gives a drier slip than beeswax, and squalane is better at preventing transepidermal water loss than dimethicone in low-humidity conditions. The formula should wear tighter and keep lips less dehydrated under matte pigment — the exact complaint the original had.

The verdict: If you abandoned MatteTrance because it turned your lips into parchment by hour three, this reformulation is worth retrying. The shade range is unchanged. The wear is reportedly better. The price is still absurd.

The switch from dimethicone to squalane means less occlusive silicone sitting on top and more actual moisture integration. That's a real upgrade for anyone whose lips dry out under matte formulas.
