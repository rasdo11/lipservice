The Injection Report
The Feds Just Seized $3M in Fake Filler at the Border

What's going on: U.S. Customs and Border Protection announced this week the seizure of over 4,500 units of counterfeit Juvederm and Restylane at a Los Angeles port — the largest single interception of fraudulent dermal fillers in agency history. The shipment, valued at roughly $3.2 million, originated from a manufacturing operation in South Korea with ties to unlicensed distributors across three states.

What it means: Counterfeit HA fillers aren't just mislabeled — they're unsterilized, inconsistently cross-linked, and sometimes cut with industrial-grade silicone. Your injector's supply chain matters as much as their technique. Board-certified doesn't help you if they're buying from a gray-market wholesaler to protect margins.

What's next: Ask your provider one question before your next appointment: *Where do you source your product?* The answer should be Allergan or Galderma direct, or an authorized distributor they can name. Hesitation is your answer.

If your filler costs dramatically less than the going rate in your market, you are not getting a deal. You are getting a mystery syringe.

Put It In Your Mouth
High-Protein Ice Cream Just Got Its First Real Clinical Win

What's going on: A randomized crossover trial published this week in *The American Journal of Clinical Nutrition* found that ice cream reformulated with 20g whey protein isolate and reduced sugar produced significantly lower postprandial glucose spikes and higher satiety scores than standard premium ice cream — even when matched for calories. The study used a Halo Top–style formulation but wasn't brand-sponsored.

What that means: The protein isn't just a marketing gimmick. The whey slows gastric emptying enough to meaningfully blunt the glucose curve, which matters if you're eating dessert after dinner and don't want to wake up puffy.

Lip Lab
Glossier Just Quietly Changed the Ultralip Formula

What's going on: Eagle-eyed Redditors on r/glossier noticed this week that Ultralip's ingredient list changed on-site — meadowfoam seed oil moved up, and synthetic beeswax replaced the natural version. Glossier hasn't announced a reformulation.

Why it matters: Meadowfoam seed oil is a superior occlusive for lip products — it mimics the skin's natural sebum layer and doesn't pill under other products. Synthetic beeswax gives a more consistent melt point, which means less textural variation batch to batch. This is quietly a better formula.

The verdict: If you bounced on Ultralip because it felt waxy or inconsistent, the current batch is worth a second pass. The Villa shade is doing something genuinely beautiful on medium skin tones.

Synthetic beeswax ≠ cheap. It actually gives formulators tighter control over texture and melt behavior. This is an upgrade disguised as a cost move.
