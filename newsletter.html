The Injection Report
Daxxify's Bulk-Buying Problem and the Fifth Toxin Nobody Saw Coming

What's going on: The FDA cleared letibotulinumtoxinA (Letybo, from Hugel) this month, making it the fifth botulinum toxin type A available in the U.S. — joining Botox, Dysport, Xeomin, and Daxxify. Meanwhile, Revance reported Q1 earnings showing Daxxify revenue up 48% year-over-year, but flagged that some practices are bulk-buying at discount and sitting on inventory rather than injecting at pace.

What it means: More toxins means more leverage for your injector to match the molecule to your anatomy — Letybo's 150 kDa size slots it closest to Xeomin (no complexing proteins), but early Korean post-market data suggests slightly faster onset. The Daxxify inventory note matters because it hints that some of those "fully booked" practices may be oversupplied, not overdemanded.

What's next: Ask your injector if they're evaluating Letybo and what their switching criteria look like. If you're a Daxxify patient, ask whether your practice is pricing based on current acquisition cost — you may have room to negotiate.

Five toxins on the U.S. market means this is officially a buyer's market — if your injector only carries one, ask why.

Put It In Your Mouth
Rice Cakes Are Glycating Your Collagen and Nobody Told You

What's going on: A study published this week in *Nutrients* measured the advanced glycation end-products (AGEs) formed during the puffing process used to make rice cakes and found levels comparable to deep-fried snacks — despite their "clean eating" reputation. The high heat and low moisture create a perfect storm for Maillard-reaction byproducts.

What that means: AGEs cross-link collagen fibers, making skin stiffer and less elastic. That "healthy" desk snack is doing more structural damage than the French fry you feel guilty about. Swap for something with fat and moisture — both of which inhibit AGE formation during cooking.

This week's cheat meal: A proper lobster roll from a place that uses too much butter. The fat is collagen-protective and you deserve the dopamine.

This Week’s Cheat Meal
A butter-drenched lobster roll — fat inhibits glycation, science said so, stop asking questions.
Low-moisture, high-heat processed snacks (rice cakes, puffed cereals, dry crackers) generate AGEs at levels most people associate only with fried food.

Lip Lab
Dior Lip Oil Gets a Ceramide Reboot — And It Actually Works

What's going on: Dior reformulated its Addict Lip Glow Oil this month, adding ceramide NP and a cherry oil complex to the existing formula. It's their first lip product to include barrier-repair lipids at a concentration they're willing to disclose (2.4%).

Why it matters: Most lip oils are just pigmented occlusive — they sit on top and look pretty. Ceramide NP actually integrates into the lipid matrix of the stratum corneum, which on lips is only about five cell layers deep. That's meaningful barrier repair, not just shine.

The verdict: Finish is glossy without the tacky pull. Pigment is sheer enough to layer. At $40, you're paying Dior tax, but the formula finally justifies it. Best for anyone whose lips crack under matte lipstick.

If your lip oil doesn't list ceramides, sphingolipids, or cholesterol esters, it's a gloss with better marketing.
