The Injection Report
Counterfeit Botox Bust Spans Five States

What's going on: The DOJ unsealed charges this week against a distribution network selling counterfeit onabotulinumtoxinA out of Houston, with seized vials traced to med spas in Texas, Florida, Arizona, Colorado, and Illinois. The products carried fake Allergan lot numbers and were sold at roughly 60% of wholesale price — which is exactly how your bargain injector keeps their margins.

What it means: Counterfeit neurotoxin isn't just ineffective — it's unregulated in potency, which means you could get zero units of actual toxin or a wildly concentrated dose. The FDA's adverse event reports from the 2024 counterfeit wave included dysphagia, drooping beyond the injection site, and hospitalization. The price gap is always the tell.

What's next: Ask your injector to show you the sealed vial before reconstitution. A legitimate practice will have no problem with this. If they balk, leave.

The one question that filters out 90% of risk: 'Can I see the sealed vial and the lot number before you reconstitute?' Any injector worth sitting in their chair will say yes without blinking.

Put It In Your Mouth
Guava Just Entered the Collagen Chat

What's going on: A study published this week in the *Journal of Nutritional Biochemistry* found that guava leaf extract significantly upregulated type I and type III collagen synthesis in human dermal fibroblasts — outperforming the vitamin C control at equivalent concentrations. The mechanism appears to be gallic acid and quercetin working synergistically on the TGF-β pathway.

What that means: You don't need the extract to benefit. Whole guava has one of the highest vitamin C densities of any fruit (228 mg per fruit vs. 70 mg in an orange) plus the polyphenol profile the study flagged. Eating the fruit gets you most of the way there.

This week's cheat meal: A guava paste and manchego quesadilla from any Cuban spot that knows what it's doing. The salty-sweet ratio is criminal and you're technically supplementing.

Lip Lab
Rare Beauty's Reformulated Lip Oil Drops the Fragrance

What's going on: Rare Beauty quietly reformulated its Soft Pinch Tinted Lip Oil this month, and the updated INCI list confirms they've dropped parfum entirely and swapped the old silicone base for a squalane-and-jojoba blend.

Why it matters: The original formula was a known irritant for anyone with fragrance sensitivity, and the silicone base sat on top of lips rather than absorbing. Squalane actually mimics the skin's own lipid profile. This is a meaningful reformulation, not a rebrand.

The verdict: Wears like a gloss, absorbs like a balm, tastes like nothing. The shade Happy finally looks like the campaign photos on actual human lips. Best in the under-$25 category right now.

If you returned the original for the fragrance, the reformulation is worth a second shot — the irritant is gone and the base actually hydrates now.
