The Injection Report
Sculptra Is the Quiet Winner of the Ozempic Face Era

What's going on: Galderma reported a 27% increase in Sculptra sales this quarter, driven almost entirely by what reps are internally calling the "GLP-1 correction" category. Dermatologists at this week's Cosmetic Bootcamp in Aspen confirmed they're using poly-L-lactic acid as a first-line treatment for the mid-face volume loss that follows rapid weight loss on semaglutide and tirzepatide.

What it means: Unlike HA fillers, Sculptra stimulates your own collagen over 2–3 sessions, which makes it better suited for diffuse volume loss across the whole face rather than spot-filling a single fold. The tradeoff is patience — full results take 3–6 months. But for GLP-1 patients who lost volume gradually, the slow rebuild actually tracks better than a sudden bolus of Voluma.

What's next: Ask your injector whether they're adjusting dilution ratios for GLP-1 patients specifically. Some are going more dilute and spreading wider. That detail matters.

If you've lost more than 15 lbs on a GLP-1 and your face looks deflated, Sculptra is worth a consult before you let anyone touch you with hyaluronic acid. Different problem, different tool.

Put It In Your Mouth
Strawberries Just Got a Clinical Glow-Up

What's going on: A study published this week in *Nutrients* found that women who ate roughly one cup of strawberries daily for 8 weeks showed measurable improvements in skin elasticity and hydration versus controls. The mechanism: ellagic acid inhibits MMP-1, the enzyme that chews through collagen after UV exposure.

What that means: This isn't a superfood grift — ellagic acid's photoprotective effects have been documented before, but this is one of the first dietary intervention trials showing skin-specific outcomes in humans at a dose you can actually eat.

This week's cheat meal: The strawberry-ricotta toast at Gjusta in Venice Beach. Sourdough, whipped ricotta, macerated strawberries, a hit of black pepper. Collagen support you can Instagram.

What to cut: Those gummy "skin vitamins" with 2mg of berry extract and 8g of sugar. You'd need 40 gummies to match one cup of actual strawberries.
