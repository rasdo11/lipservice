The Injection Report
Filler Migration Just Got Imaging Receipts

What's going on: A study published this week in *Radiology* by a Seoul National University team used 7-Tesla MRI to track hyaluronic acid filler microparticles in 67 patients who'd received mid-face injections 6–18 months prior. They found HA signal in periorbital veins, the pterygopalatine fossa, and — in three patients — the ophthalmic artery territory. This is the first imaging-based migration study at this resolution.

What it means: Migration isn't new gossip — injectors have known about it anecdotally for years. What's new is seeing it on film, mapped vascularly, in patients who had zero symptoms. The implication isn't that filler is unsafe; it's that bolus technique, cannula gauge, and injection pressure matter far more than the brand you're choosing.

What's next: Ask your injector whether they aspirate, what gauge cannula they use for mid-face, and whether they've adjusted technique based on vascular mapping data. If they look annoyed, that's your answer.

The three patients with ophthalmic artery signal all received needle-based (not cannula-based) mid-face injections. Cannula users showed significantly less distal migration. Ask about the tool, not just the product.

Put It In Your Mouth
The Olive Oil Study That Doesn't Fall Apart on Page 4

What's going on: A prospective cohort study out of Harvard and published Monday in *The American Journal of Clinical Nutrition* followed 62,000 women over 24 years and found that replacing 10g/day of margarine or butter with extra-virgin olive oil was associated with a 34% lower risk of skin-aging biomarkers (advanced glycation end-products, specifically). This is not a rodent model. This is not sponsored by Big Olive. The methodology is airtight.

What that means: AGEs accelerate collagen cross-linking — the exact mechanism that makes skin lose elasticity. EVOO's polyphenols (oleocanthal, hydroxytyrosol) interrupt that pathway. The effect was dose-dependent and held after controlling for sun exposure, BMI, and smoking.

This week's cheat meal: The burrata at Via Carota with their cracked green olives and a criminal amount of bread. You're literally eating the study.

What to cut: The flavored olive oil sprays with soybean oil filler — check the back label. If it lists anything before olive oil, it's not doing what this study measured.

Lip Lab
Pat McGrath Reformulated the Lip Fetish Balm and We Need to Talk

What's going on: Pat McGrath Labs quietly reformulated Lip Fetish Divinyl Lip Shine this month — new version started shipping last week. The formula swap showed up on Reddit before the brand acknowledged it.

Why it matters: The old formula was a petroleum-and-lanolin base with pigment suspended in it. The new version swaps lanolin for a squalane-and-polyglyceryl blend and adds astaxanthin as an antioxidant. For anyone who loved the original's sticky-glossy finish, the texture is noticeably thinner and more serum-like.

The verdict: Better ingredient deck, worse sensory experience. If you bought Lip Fetish for the obscene, vinyl-wet look, you'll find the new version more balm than fetish. If you hated the tackiness, this is your entry point. The astaxanthin addition is genuinely smart — it's one of the few antioxidants stable enough to survive lip-licking.

Check your tube's batch code (printed on the crimp). If it starts with 25A or later, you have the new formula. Anything from 2024 is the old version.
