The Injection Report
A Non-HA Filler Just Entered the Chat

What's going on: Aestheia Therapeutics submitted its BLA to the FDA this week for AES-200, a resilient elastin-like polypeptide (RELP) filler designed for midface volume. It's the first biologically derived, non-hyaluronic acid, non-collagen filler to reach this stage of review. The company says it integrates with tissue rather than sitting in a bolus, and early trial data showed duration past 18 months.

What it means: HA fillers work, but they migrate, they absorb water, and they dissolve unpredictably. A polypeptide scaffold that actually integrates could change the math on how often you're rebooking — and reduce the puffy, overfilled look that comes from HA's hydrophilic nature. The Phase III data on migration rates is what injectors are watching most closely.

What's next: FDA review will take 10–12 months minimum. In the meantime, ask your injector if they're tracking RELP technology — it tells you whether they're reading the literature or just restocking the same three syringes.

If your injector can't tell you the difference between a bolus placement and a scaffold integration, that's your sign to consult elsewhere.

Put It In Your Mouth
Ultra-Processed Food Rewires Taste in Two Weeks

What's going on: A study published this week in *Nature Metabolism* put 30 adults on a controlled ultra-processed diet for 14 days and found measurably blunted taste receptor sensitivity — specifically to sweet and umami — by day 12. Participants ate more calories not because they were hungrier, but because they literally needed more flavor to register satisfaction.

What that means: This isn't a willpower problem. Your tongue's signal-to-noise ratio is getting wrecked by engineered flavor, and two weeks is all it takes. The reversal timeline in the study was roughly three weeks of whole-food eating before sensitivity normalized.

This week's cheat meal: The strawberry pistachio croissant at Lune Croissanterie — flaky, absurd, exactly enough sweetness that your non-wrecked taste buds will actually register it.

What to cut: Flavored collagen powders. Most are loaded with the exact engineered sweeteners the study flagged. Plain collagen in coffee does the same job without dulling your palate.

Lip Lab
The Lip Oil Correction Is Here

What's going on: Glossier quietly reformulated Lip Gloss this week, shifting from a traditional polybutene-heavy formula to what they're calling a "lip oil hybrid" — but with actual occlusive staying power. Meanwhile, three indie brands (Saie, Kosas, Ilia) have all pulled or reformulated their standalone lip oils in the last 30 days.

Why it matters: The lip oil category exploded because people wanted shine without stickiness, but most formulas evaporated in 40 minutes and left lips drier than before. The market is self-correcting toward hybrid formulas that actually occlude — meaning they seal moisture in rather than just sitting on top looking pretty.

The verdict: Glossier's reformulation is genuinely better — cushiony, non-migrating, and it survived a coffee. If you've been hoarding a lip oil that disappears by lunch, this is your off-ramp.

A lip product that evaporates in under an hour isn't hydrating — it's decorative. Look for squalane, ceramides, or lanolin on the ingredient list if you want function with the shine.
