The Injection Report
Juvederm Volbella Lots Recalled — Here's What Actually Happened

What's going on: Allergan issued a voluntary recall of specific Juvederm Volbella XC lots this week after reports of particulate matter found in prefilled syringes. The affected lots were distributed between March and May 2025, and the FDA classified it as a Class II recall — meaning it could cause "temporary or medically reversible" adverse effects. If you've had lip filler in the last 90 days, this is worth a conversation.

What it means: Particulate contamination in HA fillers can trigger granulomatous reactions — your immune system walling off foreign material into hard, persistent nodules. These aren't the same as the lumps from bad placement. They're inflammatory, they're stubborn, and they sometimes need intralesional steroid or hyaluronidase to resolve. The recall is narrow, but the principle matters: always ask for the lot number.

What's next: Text your injector. Ask if your last syringe came from an affected lot — they're required to keep records. If you're booking new, ask to see the lot number before injection and photograph it for your own file.

Photograph your syringe lot number at every appointment. If a recall hits six months later, your injector's front desk won't remember — but your camera roll will.

Put It In Your Mouth
Pumpkin Seeds Are the Oral Collagen Play Nobody's Talking About

What's going on: A study published this week in *Nutrients* found that daily supplementation with pumpkin seed extract (400mg) significantly increased procollagen I levels and skin elasticity in women 35–55 over 12 weeks compared to placebo. The mechanism is the zinc-copper ratio — pumpkin seeds deliver both in proportions that support lysyl oxidase, the enzyme that crosslinks collagen fibers.

What that means: You've been buying collagen peptide powders that get digested into generic amino acids. Meanwhile, a handful of pepitas is handing your fibroblasts the cofactors they actually need to build collagen in situ. It's less glamorous. It works.

Lip Lab
Pat McGrath Quietly Reformulated MatteTrance — And Changed the Wax System

What's going on: Eagle-eyed Reddit users flagged that recent MatteTrance lipstick batches have a noticeably different texture — less drag, slightly more transfer. Pat McGrath Labs confirmed to beauty press this week that they updated the wax blend, swapping microcrystalline wax for a synthetic polyethylene base.

Why it matters: Microcrystalline wax is what gave the original its sculpted, almost-too-precise application. Polyethylene is cheaper, more stable in warm storage, and gives a softer deposit. The trade-off is longevity — early reports suggest about 30% more transfer after four hours.

The verdict: If you loved MatteTrance for its architectural hold, try before you rebuy. The new formula is more comfortable but plays a different game. Best for: anyone who found the original too stiff.

Check the batch code on the bottom of your tube. Codes starting with '25A' or later are the new formula. Anything earlier is the original wax system.
