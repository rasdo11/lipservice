The Injection Report
The GLP-1 Filler Reckoning Is Here

What's going on: A presentation at this week's Aesthetic Society meeting in Vancouver detailed emerging complications from traditional filler placement in patients who've experienced rapid facial volume loss on GLP-1 agonists like semaglutide and tirzepatide. The key issue: subcutaneous fat pads deflate unevenly, and injectors using pre-weight-loss mapping are placing product into tissue planes that have fundamentally shifted — leading to migration, visible nodularity, and that uncanny pillowed look.

What it means: Your face doesn't just lose fat uniformly. The buccal, malar, and perioral fat compartments deflate at different rates, and the connective septae between them loosen. Filler placed where fat used to be doesn't behave the same as filler placed into fat. Several panelists recommended waiting 6–9 months post-weight-stabilization before any volumizing, and one suggested biostimulators like Sculptra or Radiesse dilute as a first pass instead of HA fillers.

What's next: If you're on a GLP-1 and considering filler, ask your injector directly: have you adjusted your injection mapping for post-GLP-1 anatomy? If they blink, leave.

The question to ask before booking: "Are you placing filler based on my current anatomy or my pre-weight-loss photos?" They should be scanning your face now, not referencing what it used to look like.

Put It In Your Mouth
High-Dose Olive Oil Just Got Its Clinical Moment

What's going on: A randomized controlled trial published this week in *Nutrients* found that participants consuming 40–60 mL of extra virgin olive oil daily (roughly 3–4 tablespoons) for 12 weeks showed statistically significant improvements in skin hydration and elasticity compared to controls, with the effect most pronounced in perioral skin. The mechanism tracks: oleocanthal and hydroxytyrosol are anti-inflammatory polyphenols that support ceramide synthesis from the inside.

What that means: This isn't "olive oil is healthy" news — it's dosing data. The threshold for measurable skin benefit was 40 mL. Your sad drizzle on salad isn't cutting it. The bread-dipping, sauce-soaking, liberally-pouring approach is, per the data, the correct one.

This week's cheat meal: The lamb chops at Lilia in Brooklyn, dragged through a pool of salsa verde that is at least 60% olive oil. No math required.

Lip Lab
Pat McGrath Quietly Reformulated MatteTrance

What's going on: Pat McGrath Labs rolled out a reformulated MatteTrance lipstick this week with updated packaging and — buried in the ingredient list — a swap from synthetic beeswax to a plant-derived alternative plus the addition of squalane. The brand hasn't made a big announcement, but the INCI list changed and the internet noticed.

Why it matters: The original MatteTrance was iconic but divisive — the matte was genuinely matte, which meant flaking and feathering on anyone with even mild lip texture. Squalane in a matte formula is a signal: they're chasing comfort without surrendering opacity. The plant wax swap also makes this functionally vegan for the first time.

The verdict: If you gave up on MatteTrance because it aged your lips by ten years after hour three, this reformulation is worth a second pass. The shade Elson is still the one.

The new MatteTrance is functionally vegan and includes squalane for the first time — a comfort upgrade to a formula that desperately needed one. Shade to grab: Elson.
