The Injection Report
HA Filler and the Immune Response Nobody Warned You About

What's going on: A study published this week in *Aesthetic Surgery Journal* used PET-CT imaging to document persistent low-grade inflammatory responses around hyaluronic acid filler deposits — in some cases more than three years post-injection. The inflammatory signal was subclinical, meaning patients had no visible swelling or symptoms, but the immune system was quietly busy.

What it means: HA has always been marketed as biocompatible and fully resorbable. This doesn't disprove that, but it complicates the narrative. The researchers suggest that filler fragments, rather than intact gel, may act as damage-associated molecular patterns (DAMPs) that keep the innate immune system mildly activated. Think of it less as rejection and more as a low hum your body never stops hearing.

What's next: Ask your injector whether they've adjusted their approach to layering — stacking filler on top of old, partially degraded product is the scenario most likely to amplify this effect. Dissolving before re-treating may matter more than we thought.

If you've had filler in the same area three or more times without dissolving first, bring this study up at your next appointment. The question isn't whether to panic — it's whether your provider is keeping up.

Lip Lab
The Lip Oil Correction Is Here

What's going on: Glossier quietly reformulated Lip Gloss this week — and notably, it is not a lip oil. The brand confirmed the updated formula removes polybutene (the ingredient that made the original feel like a slip-n-slide) and adds squalane and a peptide complex instead.

Why it matters: This is the clearest signal yet that the lip oil oversaturation has peaked. Brands are pulling back toward hybrid formulas — gloss texture, treatment ingredients — rather than pure oil formats that migrate, taste weird, and don't actually occlude.

The verdict: The new Glossier formula is thinner, slightly tacky in a good way, and genuinely moisturizing at the two-hour mark. It's a gloss that acts like a balm. Best for: anyone who gave up on gloss because oil promised more and delivered less.

If your lip oil slides off within 30 minutes, it's not a treatment — it's a texture. The category correction is overdue.
