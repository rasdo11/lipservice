The Injection Report
Allergan Publishes Its First Filler Protocol for GLP-1 Volume Loss

What's going on: Allergan Aesthetics released a clinical practice bulletin this week specifically addressing facial volume loss in patients on GLP-1 receptor agonists — the first time a major manufacturer has issued formal injection guidance for so-called "Ozempic face." The bulletin recommends a phased approach: Voluma in the midface first, Volux along the jawline 4–6 weeks later, with reassessment at 12 weeks rather than the standard 6-month follow-up.

What it means: The shorter reassessment window matters. GLP-1 patients are still losing volume between sessions, so the filler is essentially settling into a moving target. The bulletin also flags that these patients may metabolize hyaluronic acid faster, meaning longevity claims on the box don't apply the way you think they do.

What's next: Ask your injector if they've seen the bulletin. If they haven't, that tells you something. The 12-week reassessment alone could save you from overcorrection down the line.

If you're on semaglutide or tirzepatide and considering filler, ask specifically about phased placement and shorter follow-up intervals — the standard protocol wasn't designed for active weight loss.

Put It In Your Mouth
High-Dose Cocoa Flavanols Just Got a Real Endorsement

What's going on: The American Journal of Clinical Nutrition published follow-up data this week from the COSMOS trial — the large-scale cocoa flavanol supplement study — showing that 500mg daily of cocoa flavanols reduced cardiovascular events by 27% in adults over 60, with the strongest effect in those with the lowest baseline flavanol intake. This is real, preregistered, 21,000-participant data.

What that means: The dose matters. You cannot eat your way there with a Hershey bar. You'd need roughly 10 tablespoons of natural cocoa powder daily to hit 500mg, or a targeted supplement like the one used in the trial (Cocoavia, which is the Mars Edge product that funded the study — disclose and move on, the data still holds).

This week's cheat meal: A double-thick dark chocolate tahini cookie from Levain Bakery's summer menu, which dropped Wednesday. It won't hit 500mg of flavanols but it will hit every other receptor that matters.

Lip Lab
Dior Lip Oil Got a Quiet Reformulation and Lost Its Best Ingredient

What's going on: Eagle-eyed reviewers on r/MakeupAddiction flagged this week that Dior Addict Lip Glow Oil has been reformulated — the new batch codes (2025 production) show cherry oil has been swapped out for a generic emollient blend. Dior hasn't announced the change.

Why it matters: Cherry seed oil was the ingredient that gave the original its non-sticky, skin-identical slip. The replacement blend (caprylic/capric triglyceride + polybutene) is cheaper and more stable, but it's the formula behind every $9 drugstore oil gloss. You're now paying $40 for a mid-tier base in luxury packaging.

The verdict: If you loved the original, check your batch code. Pre-2025 stock is still floating around at Nordstrom and Sephora. Otherwise, the Tower 28 ShineOn is doing what the old Dior did, for less.

Check the batch code on the bottom of the tube — anything starting with 4 is 2024 production (old formula). Codes starting with 5 are the new blend.
