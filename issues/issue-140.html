The Injection Report
That Filler From Five Years Ago? It's Still There.

What's going on: A study published this week in *Aesthetic Surgery Journal* out of Amsterdam UMC used MRI imaging to track hyaluronic acid filler in patients 5–10 years post-injection. The findings: residual filler was detected in 100% of participants, often in locations distant from the original injection site — migrated along tissue planes into the periorbital region and nasolabial folds.

What it means: The clinical assumption that HA filler fully dissolves within 12–18 months is now radiologically disproven. Low-grade, subclinical migration appears to be the norm, not the exception. This doesn't mean your face is in danger — but it does mean the "clean slate" your injector assumes before your next session may not exist.

What's next: If you've had repeated filler over multiple years, ask your injector whether they'd consider an MRI or ultrasound before your next round. Knowing what's already there changes the treatment plan.

Ask before your next appointment: "Are you accounting for residual filler from prior sessions, and would imaging change your approach?"

Put It In Your Mouth
Your Extra Virgin Might Not Be Extra Anything

What's going on: A UC Davis investigation updated this week found that 50–80% of imported extra virgin olive oils sold in the U.S. fail to meet international EVOO standards — many are cut with refined olive oil or seed oils like canola and sunflower. The report resurfaced after a viral TikTok sommelier blind-taste exposed three top-selling grocery brands, racking up 11M views in four days.

What that means: If you're choosing EVOO for its polyphenols, oleocanthal (the compound with anti-inflammatory properties comparable to ibuprofen), and skin-supporting fatty acid profile, an adulterated bottle delivers none of that. You're paying for capsaicin-level marketing on vegetable oil.

This week's cheat meal: A proper *pan con tomate* — crusty sourdough rubbed with ripe tomato, flaky salt, and a genuinely good olive oil you can now actually verify. The Cobram Estate California Select passed every purity test in the UC Davis panel.

Lip Lab
Glossier Reformulated Ultralip and We Have Feelings

What's going on: Glossier quietly rolled out a reformulated Ultralip this week — new packaging, updated ingredient list, same shade range. The brand confirmed the change on Instagram after users flagged a different texture.

Why it matters: The original Ultralip was a rare thing: a balm-lipstick hybrid with a clean-enough deck and genuine slip that didn't migrate. The reformulation swaps meadowfoam seed oil for a silicone-based emollient system, which improves wear time but kills the buttery mouthfeel that made it a cult product.

The verdict: If you prioritized feel over longevity, stock up on old-formula tubes at TJ Maxx while they last. If you wanted Ultralip to survive a coffee, the new version actually delivers. It's a lateral move, not an upgrade.

The old formula is already showing up at TJ Maxx and Marshalls — if you loved the original texture, this is your window.
