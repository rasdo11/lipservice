The Injection Report
Your Hyaluronic Acid Filler From 2019 Is Still There. Just Not Where You Left It.

What's going on: A new imaging study published this month in *Aesthetic Surgery Journal* used high-resolution MRI to track hyaluronic acid filler in patients who'd been injected 2–10 years prior and never had dissolving treatments. Researchers found persistent filler deposits in all 80 subjects — often migrated millimeters to centimeters from the original injection site, pooling along fascial planes and gravitational pathways.

What it means: The old line that HA filler "dissolves naturally in 6–12 months" is functionally dead. The body encapsulates residual filler in fibrous tissue rather than fully metabolizing it, and over time that capsule shifts. This is especially pronounced in the lips and nasolabial folds — high-movement areas where mechanical forces push product along tissue planes.

What's next: If you've had multiple rounds of filler without dissolving between them, ask your injector about an MRI or high-frequency ultrasound to map what's actually there before adding more. The era of "just topping off" is over.

Ask your injector: "Can we do an ultrasound assessment before my next syringe?" Any provider worth seeing will say yes.

Put It In Your Mouth
Lycopene Just Entered Its Clinical Era

What's going on: A randomized controlled trial published this week in *Nutrients* gave 60 women 30mg of lycopene daily for 12 weeks and measured skin elasticity, hydration, and UV-induced erythema against placebo. The lycopene group showed a 20% reduction in UV redness and measurable improvement in dermal density via ultrasound — the first time a lycopene-specific trial has shown structural skin changes, not just antioxidant markers in blood.

What that means: You've been told to eat tomatoes for your skin for years. Now there's dose-specific evidence: 30mg daily, which is roughly one cup of tomato paste or two large wedges of watermelon. Supplements work too, but whole-food sources come with fiber and co-factors that improve absorption.

This week's cheat meal: A fat slab of Roberta's margherita pizza — cooked San Marzano sauce is one of the most bioavailable lycopene sources on earth, and the olive oil boosts absorption. Science said so.

Lip Lab
Glossier Quietly Changed the Ultralip Formula and We Need to Talk

What's going on: Glossier updated the Ultralip ingredient list this month with no announcement — swapping synthetic beeswax for a plant-derived alternative and adding squalane higher in the formula. Eagle-eyed Reddit users caught it when their repurchase felt different on application.

Why it matters: The original Ultralip was a castor oil–heavy balm-lipstick hybrid that bled on anyone with lip texture. The reformulation is noticeably less slip, more cling. Squalane moving up the INCI list means better moisture without the slide — it mimics your skin's own lipids rather than just sitting on top.

The verdict: If you abandoned Ultralip for feathering, try it again. The new formula wears like a tinted treatment. Villa and Cachet are the shades doing the most work right now.

Check your tube's batch code — if it starts with 25A or later, you have the new formula. Night and day difference in wear.
