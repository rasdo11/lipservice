The Injection Report
Allergan Rewrote the Allē Math — Here's What It Costs You

What's going on: Allergan Aesthetics rolled out a restructured Allē loyalty program this week, adjusting point-to-dollar conversion rates and adding new tier thresholds. The net effect: points earned on Botox and Juvéderm treatments now redeem at roughly 15–20% less value than they did under the previous structure. Existing points are honored but under the new conversion. What it means: If you've been banking points for a big treatment — think a full syringe of Volux or a Botox top-up — the redemption window just got less generous. Allergan frames this as "simplification," but the economics tilt toward more frequent, smaller redemptions rather than stockpiling. Your injector likely got the memo last month; most patients are finding out now. What's next: Ask your provider's office whether they're offering any bridge promotions or in-house credits to offset the gap. Some practices are eating the difference to keep patients from shopping around.

Before your next appointment, log into Allē and check your current point balance against the updated redemption chart — you may want to redeem now rather than let the new math erode your stash.

Put It In Your Mouth
High-Dose Omega-3s and the Inflammation Your Lips Feel First

What's going on: A randomized trial published this week in *Nutrients* found that 12 weeks of high-dose EPA/DHA supplementation (2.4g/day) significantly reduced serum markers of systemic inflammation — including CRP and IL-6 — in women aged 35–55. The kicker: participants self-reported measurable improvements in perioral dryness and lip cracking, a secondary endpoint no one expected to hit significance. What that means: Chronic low-grade inflammation shows up at mucosal boundaries first — your lips, your gut lining, your under-eyes. This trial suggests that at therapeutic doses, omega-3s may do more for your lip barrier than another balm layer. This week's cheat meal: The lobster roll at Luke's, or your local equivalent — wild-caught lobster is one of the densest whole-food EPA sources, and drawn butter never needed an apology.

Lip Lab
Merit Shade Slick Got a Quiet Reformulation — and It's Better

What's going on: Merit's Shade Slick Lip Oil, the understated-luxury crowd's daily driver, shipped a reformulated version this week with updated ingredient lists appearing on Sephora and Merit's DTC site. The change: meadowfoam seed oil replaces the previous coconut-derived emollient base, and they've upped the squalane concentration. Why it matters: Meadowfoam has a longer oxidative stability than coconut derivatives, meaning the product stays fresh longer after opening and lays down a more even film. Squalane at higher concentrations improves glide without the tackiness that plagues most glossy oils. The verdict: Slightly thinner viscosity, better wear, same barely-there tint range. If you bounced off the original for being too sticky, this version fixes that. Best on: anyone who wants a gloss that doesn't announce itself.

The reformulated version has a batch code starting with '25A' on the crimp — check yours before assuming you've already tried the new formula.
