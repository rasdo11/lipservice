The Injection Report
A Faster, Cleaner Filler Eraser Just Cleared Phase 3

What's going on: Revance disclosed top-line Phase 3 results this week for RVT-1502, an engineered recombinant hyaluronidase designed to dissolve HA fillers faster and with fewer immune reactions than the current standard (Hylenex). The trial hit its primary endpoint — complete filler resolution at 36 hours — with a significantly lower incidence of the swelling cascade that makes current dissolutions so miserable.

What it means: Right now, dissolving filler is imprecise. Hyaluronidase is bovine-derived, dosing is vibes-based, and overcorrection is common. A purpose-built recombinant enzyme with cleaner pharmacokinetics could make reversals predictable rather than dramatic. Your injector would actually know what's going to happen.

What's next: FDA submission is expected Q1 2026. If you're sitting on filler you regret, this is worth waiting for — ask your injector whether they're tracking RVT-1502 and what their current dissolution protocol looks like.

Ask your injector: "What enzyme do you use for dissolutions, and how do you dose it?" If the answer is vague, that tells you something.

Put It In Your Mouth
Cardiologists Just Gave Cocoa Flavanols a Real Cosign

What's going on: The American Journal of Clinical Nutrition published a large preregistered trial this week showing 500mg/day of cocoa flavanols reduced cardiovascular events by 27% in adults over 60 — and improved endothelial function markers that correlate with skin microcirculation. This isn't chocolate marketing. This is a 21,000-person dataset with a four-year follow-up.

What that means: Better blood flow to the microvasculature means better nutrient delivery to the dermis. The dose that moved the needle is roughly what you'd get from a concentrated flavanol supplement — not from a Hershey bar.

This week's cheat meal: The salted tahini chocolate chunk cookies at Levain's summer menu. Dense, molten center, zero regret. You earned this.

Lip Lab
Dior Lip Oil Got a Quiet Reformulation and We Have Questions

What's going on: Side-by-side INCI comparisons circulating on r/MakeupAddiction this week confirm Dior Addict Lip Glow Oil has been reformulated — cherry seed oil moved down the list, a new silicone emollient moved up, and the texture is noticeably thinner.

Why it matters: Reformulations without announcement are legal but shady. The original formula's slip and cushion came from its oil-heavy base. The new version performs more like a gloss with oil marketing. At $40, you deserve to know what changed.

The verdict: If you loved the original, stock up on old-batch inventory (check batch codes at checkcosmetic.net). The new formula isn't bad — it's just a different product wearing the same name.
